# Compliance screening

**Lead with this limit:** MORVS screens are research aids with partial, variable coverage. A non-match, low score, or failed experimental tool is not sanctions clearance, compliance approval, or a legal conclusion. Verify identity and current status in the official source before any action.

## Workflow

1. Keep scope to organizations and institution-level facts; do not reproduce personal identifiers, addresses, or complaint narratives containing PII. Resolve legal names and aliases with `resolve` using `{"q":"Organization name"}` or a known ticker or CIK. Record unresolved name collisions.
2. For a single organization, call `sanctions_check` with `{"name":"Exact legal name"}` and `debarments_check` with the same name. The sanctions tool searches a partial local OFAC SDN index by substring, including indexed aliases. Debarments covers local SAM Exclusions and HHS OIG LEIE records. Treat hits as possible matches pending identifier and current official-list review.
3. When broader context is requested, call `screen_all` with `{"name":"Exact legal name"}` if available. It is experimental; its 0–100 score and per-source counts are indicative local-index outputs. For up to 50 names call `screen_batch` with `{"names":["Name A","Name B"]}`. For two entities call `compare_entities` with `{"a":"Name A","b":"Name B"}`. Describe any ranking as a MORVS heuristic, not a determination of risk or wrongdoing.
4. Use `doj_by_company` with `{"name":"Exact legal name"}` to inspect DOJ press-release mentions and `cfpb_by_company` with the same argument to inspect a partial CFPB complaint index. A press mention need not be an enforcement action; a complaint is a consumer report, not a proven violation.
5. Inspect MORVS coverage and call `dataset_freshness` with `{}` for relevant federal hub rows. Verify material hits and negatives against current official [OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-list-service), [SAM.gov](https://sam.gov/content/exclusions), [HHS OIG](https://exclusions.oig.hhs.gov/), [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/news), or [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/) records as applicable. Preserve record IDs, dates, list version or access date, and exact source URLs. Say when current official verification was unavailable.
6. Put the caveat before the results, then provide a compact table. Report no-hit only as “no match in the checked local index on the access date.” Avoid accusations and definitive clearance language.

| Checked on | Organization and identifier | Source checked | Local result | Official record/date/URL | Match and coverage limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UTC date | Legal name plus verified ID | Agency and dataset | Hit, no local hit, or unavailable | Exact record or list URL | Alias/identity and freshness status |

## Drone screening

For drone makers, components or federal drone purchases, use the two Drone Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published register of US government drone approval, restriction and compliance records (DCMA Blue UAS Cleared List and Framework, FCC UAS Conditional Approvals, FAA Remote ID declarations, and the drone-linked entries of the Section 1260H list, the BIS Entity List and OFAC's NS-CMIC list). Call `drone_company_check` with `{"name":"DJI"}`; names and aliases match regardless of case, punctuation or legal suffix, and substrings are allowed. Each match gives the company page, approved and restricted flags, every designation with its list, action, product, date, source URL and evidence, attached reference records, and federal contract totals once loaded. Call `drone_product_lookup` with `{"q":"Mavic 3"}` to find a model or component across the Blue UAS lists, FCC conditional approvals and FAA declarations; its "context" field restates the current FCC Covered List exemption from the register's own reference records. Companies and agencies only: the register is not a registry of drone owners or pilots.

A non-match is reported exactly as "not found in the Drone Register (covers … as of <date>)". That describes one compiled dataset on one date. It is never clearance, approval or evidence that a company is unrestricted, and an FAA Remote ID declaration is the manufacturer's own statement, not an approval. Quote the "coverage_note" field with the result, and verify any hit or miss at the official lists the response returns in "official_lists": the [DCMA Blue UAS list portal](https://tyrionprod.servicenowservices.com/gsp?id=cleared_list), the [FCC Covered List page](https://www.fcc.gov/supplychain/coveredlist) and its [UAS public notice](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf), the [FAA Remote ID declarations](https://uasdoc.faa.gov/listDocs), the [Section 1260H notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11571/notice-of-availability-of-designation-of-chinese-military-companies), the [BIS Entity List](https://www.bis.gov/sites/default/files/documents/entity-list.csv) and the [OFAC sanctions list service](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-list-service). If either tool reports the register as temporarily unavailable, say so; do not treat that as a non-match.

## Data broker screening

For whether a business has registered as a data broker, use the two Data Broker Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published California Data Broker Register (the 2026, 2025 and 2024 registries filed with the California Privacy Protection Agency and the Attorney General's 2020–2023 list, keyed by website domain so a name change at the same domain reads as one history). Call `data_broker_check` with a business name, `{"name":"Acxiom"}`, a website domain or URL, `{"domain":"acxiom.com"}`, or both. Matching is exact after normalisation (case, punctuation, accents and legal suffix ignored; "Dun and Bradstreet" finds "Dun & Bradstreet, Inc."), not substring, and several matching brokers come back as separate candidates, never merged. Each match carries the broker page, its name history across vintages, "registered_current", and every registration as filed: what the broker says it collects, whom it reports having sold or shared data to, the laws it says regulate it, and its own consumer-request counts. A flag is the broker's own answer to the registration form's question, so report it as what the broker filed, not as a fact MORVS established. Call `data_broker_diff` with `{"year_a":"2025","year_b":"2026"}` to compare two files under the register's published method; it returns the counts and the named registrations that re-registered at the same domain, were not re-registered under either name or domain, or are net new, plus "published_drift" for consecutive files.

Registration is self-reported (a business filed and paid under the Delete Act) and California-only (only businesses that filed there), so absence is not a finding. A non-match is reported exactly as "No registration in the California Data Broker Registry (2026, 2025, 2024) or the Attorney General's 2020–2023 list matches '<query>' by registrable domain or normalised legal name, as published by CalPrivacy and read by MORVS on <date>. This is not a finding that the business is not a data broker, is exempt from registration, or has never registered: registration is self-reported, names and domains change, and the registry lists only businesses that filed." Never shorten that to "not a data broker", and a registration absent from a later file is "not re-registered under its name or domain", not an exit, a merger or a finding. Quote the "coverage_note" and "as_of" fields with the result, and verify any hit or miss at the [CPPA Data Broker Registry](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_broker_registry/). Businesses only: never query a person's name. A query shaped like a personal name is refused with "This register holds businesses only; queries shaped like a personal name are not run." and "refused": true; that refusal is not a non-match. If either tool reports the register as temporarily unavailable, say so; do not treat that as a non-match.

## Forced labor screening

For whether a company or facility is named in a US forced-labor action, use the two Forced Labor Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published [U.S. Forced Labor Company Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/forced-labor-register/): every organisation on the DHS UFLPA Entity List or in a CBP withhold release order or finding, the Consolidated Screening List (Commerce, Treasury) rows that exactly match one of those names, and issuer relationships a US-listed company states in its own SEC filing. Call `forced_labor_company_check` with `{"name":"Baoding LYSZD Trade and Business Co., Ltd."}`. Matching is exact on the register's name key (case, accents, punctuation and legal suffix ignored) or on a source-stated or reviewed alias (DHS "also known as" / "formerly known as", CBP "a/k/a"); there is no fuzzy, family-name or transliteration match, and several matching entities come back as separate candidates, never merged. Each match carries its records one per source row, with the list's own action, status and date, the source URL, the row locator and the snapshot date, plus "source_status" for each source. Call `forced_labor_source_compare` with `{"source_a":"dhs","source_b":"cbp"}` (sources dhs, cbp or csl) for the entities named in both sources under the register's reviewed joins, those in one source with no reviewed match in the other, the counts beside the register's own "published" figures and the source rows behind each listed id, in pages of up to 50 ids continued with "next_cursor".

UFLPA listing, a CBP withhold release order or finding, and an export-control or sanctions row are separate actions under separate laws: report each record as its own list states it, never one as evidence of another, and a source with no reviewed match reads "No reviewed name or alias match in this source snapshot", which is not a clearance finding. Issuer relationships (on by default; "include_related": false drops them) are what a US-listed company states in its own SEC filing; they never extend an action to a parent, and the response says "The parent is not itself named in these records." when that is so. A non-match is reported exactly as "No reviewed name or alias match in the MORVS source snapshots as of <date>. This does not establish that the company is cleared, absent from other records, or outside any law." Never shorten that to "cleared", "not listed" or "no forced labor". Quote the "coverage_note" and "snapshot_at" fields with the result, include the "cbp_attribution" line when CBP rows are shown, and verify any hit or miss at the [DHS UFLPA Entity List](https://www.dhs.gov/uflpa-entity-list), the [CBP withhold release orders and findings](https://www.cbp.gov/document/stats/withhold-release-orders-findings) and the [Consolidated Screening List](https://www.trade.gov/consolidated-screening-list). Organisations only: never query a person's name. A query shaped like a personal name, an email address or a phone number is refused with "This register holds organisations only; queries shaped like a personal name, an email address or a phone number are not run." and "refused": true; that refusal is not a non-match. If either tool reports the register as temporarily unavailable, say so; do not treat that as a non-match.

## Worked examples

**One vendor.** For “Screen Microsoft for sanctions and exclusions”, call `resolve` with `{"ticker":"MSFT"}`, `sanctions_check` with `{"name":"Microsoft Corporation"}`, and `debarments_check` with the same name. Confirm any candidate match at current OFAC, SAM.gov, or HHS OIG official records before reporting it. If there is no local hit, report only the local-index result and its coverage limit.

**Two counterparties.** For “Compare Pfizer and Merck”, call `compare_entities` with `{"a":"Pfizer","b":"Merck"}`; then call `sanctions_check` and `debarments_check` separately for each verified legal name. Use `doj_by_company` and `cfpb_by_company` only for relevant context, opening the original records. Explain that the comparison score is a heuristic and that name ambiguity or source lag can change it.

**Forced labor.** For “Is Baoding LYSZD on a forced-labor list?”, call `forced_labor_company_check` with `{"name":"Baoding LYSZD Trade and Business Co., Ltd."}`; it is named by both DHS and CBP, so report its UFLPA Entity List rows and its CBP withhold release order as separate records, each with its own date, status and source row. For `{"name":"Xinjiang Daqo New Energy"}`, report the UFLPA rows and the matched BIS Entity List row, then the subsidiary relationship its US-listed parent states in its own 20-F as that filing's statement with its "parent_note", never as an action against the parent. For a name with no match, such as `{"name":"Example Textiles Co., Ltd."}`, quote the non-match sentence in full. To size the overlap between agencies, call `forced_labor_source_compare` with `{"source_a":"dhs","source_b":"cbp"}` and give the counts with the snapshot date; an entity with no reviewed match in the other source is not cleared by that agency.
