# Public records research

Build a source-linked, institution-level account from MORVS public datasets. Treat a name match as a lead until an identifier and underlying record support it.

## Workflow

1. Define the organization, jurisdiction, period, and question. Avoid collecting or repeating personal identifiers, residential addresses, or other PII. Report institution-level facts.
2. Call `resolve` with exactly one known identifier when possible: `{"ticker":"PFE"}`, `{"cik":"0000078003"}`, or `{"q":"Pfizer"}`. Inspect canonical name, aliases, identifiers, available sources, and source links. Disambiguate similar names before proceeding.
3. Select the narrowest useful tool:
   - `entity_timeline` with `{"key":"PFE","days":180,"limit":100}` for a dated cross-source event sequence. The key accepts ticker, CIK, UEI, LEI, DUNS, or numeric entity ID; maximum days is 730.
   - `entity_material_events` with `{"key":"PFE","days":180}` for the high-signal subset. Its importance threshold is a MORVS selection rule, not an official finding.
   - `dossier` with `{"ticker":"PFE"}` or `{"name":"Pfizer"}` for the cross-topic overview.
   - `company_profile` with `{"ticker_or_cik":"PFE"}` for an SEC-filing company profile. Do not use its absence to infer that a private organization has no records.
4. Check the returned period, per-source coverage, record dates, and source links. Call `dataset_freshness` with `{}` if using a federal hub dataset and match its row to the dataset; distinguish source-observation time from indexed-content watermark. Mark unknown or stale coverage plainly. A missing hit is not evidence of no activity.
5. Open the primary record URL for material claims when available. Distinguish a filing, complaint, press release, recall, contract, and agency finding. State only what the source establishes. Never turn a mention, allegation, or automated flag into an accusation.
6. Return a short answer followed by a record table. Give access date and source date; omit a claim when no verifiable record URL is available.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Use the record's date | State only the supported fact | Name the agency and MORVS index | If supplied | Link to the exact record | State the source window and any gap |

## PFAS in drinking water

For what EPA's PFAS monitoring found at a public water system, use the three PFAS Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published [PFAS Drinking-Water Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/pfas/): every system in EPA's final UCMR 5 monitoring file, its six regulated PFAS averaged by sampling location with EPA's Table 4 method, and the 2024 limits at least one of those averages meets the comparison cutoff for, as versioned rule records. Call `pfas_system_lookup` with exactly one of `{"pwsid":"IA9778054"}`, `{"zip":"51106"}` or `{"state":"IA","name":"Sioux City"}`; each match carries the PWSID, ECHO population served, owner type, the six derived flags and "exceeds_rules". A ZIP is a service-area hint, not a confirmed supplier: it returns every system that reported serving part of that ZIP, so quote its "note" (check the bill or annual water quality report to confirm the supplier) and never pick one system for the user. Call `pfas_system_history` with `{"pwsid":"IA9778054"}` for one system's sampling locations, sample dates, complete-set averages in ppt, per-chemical detection counts and highest single results, and each 2024 rule record with this system's comparison in "this_system". Call `pfas_rule_impact` with `{"scenario":"if_proposals_final","state":"IA"}` (scenario "2024_as_published" or "if_proposals_final") for the systems and ECHO population served with an average at or above a scenario limit's cutoff, beside the register's "published" figures, and "lose_all_federal_exceedances": systems above only limits EPA proposed to rescind, quoted with its definition.

These are EPA UCMR 5 entry-point monitoring averages compared with the 2024 limits by EPA's method: each result is "not a compliance determination or a household tap result", and every answer says "not_compliance": true. Report a result as "at least one complete-set sampling-location average at or above the comparison cutoff" for a named limit, with the sample dates, never as a violation, and never call a system's water safe, unsafe or contaminated; "sample_pfoa_pfos_gt4" is a single sample, not an average. The comparison is federal-only: state PFAS limits are outside the register. The six 2024 limits are final and in force; EPA's May 2026 documents proposing to rescind the PFHxS, PFNA, HFPO-DA and Hazard Index limits and to extend the PFOA and PFOS deadline are proposals, not final rules, so quote each rule's "status_label". The proposed April 26, 2031 date would apply only to systems that request and are granted it: never assign 2031 to any named system. A null flag or "above_cutoff" means no complete set for that comparison, not a result below the limit, and a PWSID or ZIP not in the register "says nothing about a system's water" (most small systems and all private wells are outside UCMR 5). A system whose name the register withholds is shown by PWSID and city; report it that way. Quote the "caveat", "vintage" and "source_dates" fields with the result and point to EPA's [UCMR 5 occurrence data](https://www.epa.gov/dwucmr/occurrence-data-unregulated-contaminant-monitoring-rule) and the system's annual water quality report for verification. If any tool reports the register as temporarily unavailable, say so; do not treat that as a non-match.

## Government requests for user data

For how often governments ask a company for user data, use the two Government Data Demand Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published [Government Data Demand Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/data-demands/): the figures companies (Google, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Ring, TikTok, Snap, Discord, Reddit, X, Cloudflare, GitHub, Dropbox, OpenAI, Anthropic, Verizon, AT&T, Comcast and others) and US government bodies (the US Courts Wiretap Report, ODNI, DOJ and the FISA courts report) publish, one fact per printed value. Call `data_demand_source` with `{"source":"verizon"}` for one publisher's latest complete report (add `"period":"2025-H2"`, `"geo":"US"`, `"process":"search_warrant"` or `"measure":"requests"` to narrow it); the answer carries the report's scope, stage (received, responded, processed, authorized), notes, the printed totals it checked, the publisher's status and its access note. Call `data_demand_compare` with `{"sector":"big_tech"}` (big_tech, platform, ai, carrier, cameras_devices, official) or no arguments for every publisher's latest headline figures side by side, each with its plain label.

Each figure is that publisher's own count for its own period and scope. Companies count different things (requests, accounts, demands, orders) and differ on whether emergency, civil or preservation requests are in a total, so never add figures across companies, never rank companies, and quote "comparable_across_sources": false and the caveat when you set two side by side; compare a company with itself over time instead, and mention any series break in the report notes. A missing figure means not reported, not zero; national-security figures are bands, quoted as printed ("250-499"), never a midpoint; a figure marked derived is a MORVS sum of the publisher's own separate rows. These are aggregate counts: they cannot show whether any particular person's data was requested, so never answer that question with them. A publisher marked blocked or unpublished has no figures in the register; quote its "access_note". Link the report URL with each figure. If a tool reports the register as temporarily unavailable, say so; do not treat that as a missing figure.

## Worked examples

**A public company's recent regulatory history.** For “What happened at Pfizer over the last six months?”, call `resolve` with `{"ticker":"PFE"}`, then `entity_timeline` with `{"key":"PFE","days":180,"limit":100}` and `entity_material_events` with `{"key":"PFE","days":180}`. Use `company_profile` with `{"ticker_or_cik":"PFE"}` only if financial and SEC context matters. Reconcile duplicates by record ID and date; cite each original filing or agency record. Do not infer misconduct from a high importance score.

**An ambiguous organization name.** For “Research Acme Health”, call `resolve` with `{"q":"Acme Health"}`. Present candidate organizations and identifiers if more than one appears. After a verified match, call `dossier` with the exact organization name and `entity_timeline` with its returned identifier. If no identifier resolves, give only clearly attributed search leads and say the match is unconfirmed.

**PFAS in a town's water.** For “What PFAS did EPA find in my town's water?”, ask for the town and state or a ZIP if not given, then call `pfas_system_lookup` with `{"state":"IA","name":"Sioux City"}` or `{"zip":"51106"}`. If several systems match, list them and let the user confirm their supplier from a bill or water quality report. For the confirmed PWSID, call `pfas_system_history` with `{"pwsid":"IA9778054"}` and report which of the six PFAS were detected, the highest averages with their sample dates, and each 2024 limit's comparison with its rule status, with the monitoring-not-compliance caveat first. For “What changes if EPA's proposals become final?”, call `pfas_rule_impact` with `{"scenario":"if_proposals_final","state":"IA"}` and with `{"scenario":"2024_as_published","state":"IA"}`, and give both counts with the "generated" date, the scenario label and the rule statuses; a system in "lose_all_federal_exceedances" is described by that field's definition, not as a finding about its water.

**How often police ask for data.** For “How often do police ask Google for user data?”, call `data_demand_source` with `{"source":"google","geo":"US"}` and report the US requests by legal process (subpoenas, search warrants, emergency requests) for the latest period with its label, the share where some data was disclosed, and the report link; add that preservation requests are counted separately. For “Which phone companies report the most requests?”, call `data_demand_compare` with `{"sector":"carrier"}`, give each carrier's own figure with its label and period, say they count differently so the figures cannot be ranked, and note that T-Mobile blocks public-records access so it has no figures.

## License plate reader decisions

For what a city or county decided about Flock or other license plate reader cameras, use the two License Plate Reader Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published [License Plate Reader Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/plate-readers/): official council and board records from Legistar, state ALPR laws read from official texts, federal contract actions and official agency counts. Call `plate_reader_decisions` with `{"state":"WI"}`, `{"jurisdiction":"Denver"}`, `{"vendor":"Flock Safety","year":"2026"}` or `{"query":"terminate"}`; each decision carries the motion as recorded (approved, approved as amended, motion did not carry, filed, referred and so on), its date and body, the vendors and dollar figures printed in the title, and the government record link. Call `plate_reader_state_law` with `{"state":"VA"}` for a state's law card with citations and its verification level.

Report an outcome as the motion as recorded: an approval is an authorization, not proof a contract was signed or cameras installed; a motion that did not carry does not mean a contract ended; an amount is the printed figure, often a ceiling, never money spent. A jurisdiction with no row means no qualifying action was found in the Legistar sources checked, not that it has no cameras. A state marked not verified is "not verified", never "no law". Never say a city "banned" or "canceled" Flock unless the record's own title says so, and link the record.

## Lobbying on AI, surveillance and privacy (Lobbying Disclosure Act)

For who lobbies Congress and agencies on AI, surveillance or privacy, use `lobbying_client`, which answers from MORVS's published [Influence Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/influence/): Lobbying Disclosure Act reports from lda.gov whose activity sections name those topics. Call it with `{"client":"Anthropic"}` or `{"client":"Flock Safety"}` for each quarter's reports, topics, the in-house expense figure (with its reporting method) and the income outside lobbying firms reported. Never add in-house expenses to firm income; both cover the whole report, all issues, so never call either "spending on AI". A missing client is not proof it did no lobbying. Quote the retrieval date and the Senate Office of Public Records disclaimer the tool returns.

## Court-ordered wiretaps and national-security surveillance counts

For court-authorized wiretaps, use `wiretap_orders`, which answers from MORVS's [Surveillance Orders Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/surveillance-orders/): every order the federal judiciary published in its annual Wiretap Reports since the 2016 report. Call it with `{"report_year":2025,"state":"CALIFORNIA"}` or `{"court":"federal","offense_group":"drugs"}`. A row is an order as reported in that report year; the year's headline count leaves out wiretaps reported later, so use the register's "total authorized by year" for comparisons. Never add people or intercepts across orders (related orders share results). For Section 702, FISA orders, National Security Letters and border device searches, call `national_security_surveillance` with `{"series":"section_702_targets"}`; each figure carries the agency's own label, page and quote. These series count different things under different laws and are never added.

## Judgment Fund payments (US Treasury)

For what the federal government paid out on judgments and settlements, use `judgment_fund_payments` and `judgment_fund_payee`, which answer from MORVS's [Judgment Fund Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/judgment-fund/). Call `judgment_fund_payments` with `{"fy":2026,"agency":"Ocean Energy"}` or `judgment_fund_payee` with `{"payee":"Bluepoint"}`. A payment is not an admission of fault; Treasury's citation is its classification of the legal basis. A report line is not a settlement (big payments are split into $99,999,999.99 lines). Treasury's bi-weekly reports and its annual report to Congress are compared, never added. Individuals are never named: say "redacted by Treasury" or "name withheld", and never try to identify them.

## Federal mass lawsuits (multidistrict litigation)

For federal mass lawsuits against a company or product, use `mdl_lookup`, which answers from MORVS's [Mass Lawsuit Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/mass-litigation/): the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation's monthly reports. Call it with `{"query":"3047"}`, `{"query":"social media"}` or `{"query":"Uber"}`. Counts are pending lawsuits (actions), not people; centralisation by the Panel is not a finding on the merits; a falling count is not a settlement, because the Panel does not say why actions leave a docket. Name a company only where the Panel's caption names it.

## Police forfeiture money (DOJ equitable sharing)

For how much federal forfeiture money a police or sheriff's agency received and what it certified it spent, use `forfeiture_agency` and `forfeiture_payments`, which answer from MORVS's [Forfeiture Money Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/forfeiture-money/). Call `forfeiture_agency` with `{"state":"CA","agency":"Los Angeles Police"}`, or `forfeiture_payments` with `{"fy":2025,"state":"TX"}`. DOJ payments use the federal fiscal year; an agency's ESAC certifications use its own fiscal year and include Treasury money, so never add or subtract the two. A certified balance is not misconduct. A DOJ status such as Non-Compliant is DOJ's label, and DOJ does not publish the reason. A seizure is not a crime or a conviction. Credit the Institute for Justice's *Policing for Profit* for analysis.

## Self-driving and driver-assist crash reports (NHTSA)

For crashes involving robotaxis or Level 2 driver assistance, use `self_driving_company`, `self_driving_reports` and `nhtsa_automation_investigations`, which answer from MORVS's [Self-Driving Crash Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/self-driving/): companies' reports under NHTSA's Standing General Order, plus the NHTSA investigations and recalls MORVS reviewed by hand. Call them with `{"company":"Waymo"}`, `{"kind":"ADS","year_received":2026}` or `{"status":"open"}`. Write "the company reported a crash to NHTSA" and "highest injury severity alleged", never "caused" or "at fault". Counts are reports, not rates: they reflect fleet size and the reporting rules, so never rank companies by safety. Keep ADS and Level 2 apart. Several reports can describe one crash. Mark the June 16, 2025 rule change. Opening an investigation is not a finding.

## Prediction markets (CFTC event-contract filings)

For what prediction-market exchanges such as Kalshi and Polymarket US filed with the CFTC, use `prediction_market_filings` and `prediction_market_exchange`, which answer from MORVS's [Prediction Market Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/prediction-markets/). Call them with `{"exchange":"KEX"}` or `{"exchange":"Polymarket"}`. A "Certified" filing was self-certified by the exchange under CFTC rule 40.2, never "approved by the CFTC". Filings are not markets, volume or odds; dates are as listed by the CFTC and can re-date; topics are MORVS's classification. Titles that name a person are withheld and never searchable. Never write "bets", "gambling", "legal" or "illegal" in your own voice.

## China's AI filing lists (CAC) and US screening lists

For what Beijing's internet regulator lists as filed or registered AI services and algorithms, use `china_ai_filings` and `china_ai_us_lists`, which answer from MORVS's [China AI Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/china-ai/). Call them with `{"q":"DeepSeek"}` or `{"tier":"A"}`. Use the CAC's words, "completed filing (备案)" and "registered (登记)", never "approved". Filings are not models or companies. A brand in a filer's legal name is not proof of ownership. The Entity List is an export-licence requirement and NS-CMIC an investment ban; never say "sanctioned". Tier B entries are possible matches, not confirmed, and "not on a US list" never means "cleared". Never say a CAC DeepSeek filer is the company DHS named.

## Voting equipment (EAC certification and the 2024 survey)

For which voting systems the US Election Assistance Commission certifies and what equipment each election jurisdiction reported in 2024, use `election_voting_systems` and `election_equipment_jurisdiction`, which answer from MORVS's [Election Equipment Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/election-equipment/). Call them with `{"list":"certified"}` or `{"state":"GA","jurisdiction":"Fulton"}`. Jurisdiction equipment is self-reported for November 2024, not what is used today; point users to their state election office and Verified Voting for current equipment. Quote the EAC's statement that all EAC-certified voting systems are secure with certification answers. End-of-life retirement is proposed until the EAC removes a system. Never say "insecure", "outdated" or "decertified" (except the EAC's own list name), and never "X counted votes"; vendor counts overlap and are never added.

## Data breach notices (Washington, Delaware and California attorneys general)

For breach notices organisations filed with state attorneys general, use `breach_notices` and `breach_notice_organization`, which answer from MORVS's [Data Breach Notice Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/breach-notices/). Call them with `{"state":"WA","organization":"Conduent"}` or `{"organization":"TransUnion"}`. Timing is by each record's own dates against that state's statute, for Washington and Delaware only; the records do not show permitted delays (for example at law enforcement's request) or any enforcement outcome, so never say "late" or "violated". Counts are notices sent to residents, not people, and are never added across states. When two states' discovery dates differ, say the records carry different dates. Absence from these three states never means no breach.

## DOJ's sanctuary list and state ICE-transfer reports

For DOJ's list of "sanctuary jurisdictions" and what California, Illinois and Oregon agencies report about immigration detainers, requests and transfers, use `sanctuary_doj_list` and `sanctuary_state_reports`, which answer from MORVS's [Sanctuary Records Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/sanctuary/). Call them with `{"q":"Minnesota"}` or `{"state":"CA","agency":"Los Angeles"}`. "Sanctuary" is DOJ's label; never use it as a fact in your own voice. Each state's measure is different (California transfers, Illinois detainers and requests, Oregon requests) and is never added, ranked or compared across states. Figures are agency self-reports; "no transfers reported" or "did not file" never means "no cooperation", and never say "violated" or "illegal".

## DOJ police grants (COPS award lists)

For DOJ's Community Oriented Policing Services awards to police agencies, use `police_grant_awards` and `police_grant_recipient`, which answer from MORVS's [Police Grant Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/police-grants/). Call them with `{"fy":2026}` or `{"recipient":"Chicago"}`. Awards are DOJ's announced lists, not spending. The lists do not record scoring, so never say an award was won or lost because of immigration posture, and never "rewarded" or "punished". COPS awards, JAG allocations and forfeiture payments are never added together. No COPS award in these lists never means no federal money.

## CBP use of force and assaults on officers

For Customs and Border Protection's own monthly use-of-force and assaults-on-officers figures, use `border_force_area` and `border_force_national`, which answer from MORVS's [Border Force Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/border-force/). Call them with `{"area":"Grand Forks","fiscal_year":2026}` or `{}`. Attribute every number to CBP's files. Officers assaulted are counted per incident, not people injured. Use-of-force totals include vehicle and vessel force; show the force type. A sector or field office is never a city or an operation. No rates per encounter and no causation; a revision is a version "as published on" a date, not a correction of misconduct.

## Ukraine aid money (federal records)

For US Ukraine-response money, use `ukraine_money_totals` and `ukraine_money_recipient`, which answer from MORVS's [Ukraine Money Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/ukraine-money/). Call them with `{"view":"stages"}` or `{"q":"Lockheed"}`. Keep made available, obligated and disbursed separate and dated; never add stages. Pentagon contracts that mention Ukraine are not tied to the supplemental appropriations. For contractors, the contracts, their PACs' contributions to candidate committees (named as the FEC lists them) and their lobbying filings sit side by side: corporate PAC money is voluntary, not treasury or contract money, and the records do not show that one caused another. Never say "missing", "stolen", "funneled" or "kickback"; such words appear only inside exact quoted oversight findings.

## ICE and CBP contractors: contracts, PAC money and lobbying

For immigration-enforcement contractors, use `pay_to_play_contractor`, `pay_to_play_totals`, `pay_to_play_committees` and `pay_to_play_awards`, which answer from MORVS's [Pay-to-Play Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/pay-to-play/). Call them with `{"company":"GEO Group"}`, `{"component":"CBP","fy":2026}`, `{"cycle":2026,"company":"GEO"}` or `{"component":"ICE","fy":2026}`. The register's name labels a question people ask; it is not a finding. Contract obligations (USAspending), connected-PAC contributions to candidate committees (FEC, committees named as the FEC lists them) and lobbying reports (LDA) are three separate columns: never add them, never present them as a sequence, and never say a contribution or lobbying report caused, secured or influenced an award. Corporate PAC money is voluntary money from people the PAC may solicit, not company treasury or contract money, and PAC totals are company-wide. A company with no PAC or LDA filing on record is a search result, not a fact about the company. Appropriated, obligated and outlaid are separate stages. Never say "bought", "kickback", "rewarded" or "pay-to-play" as a claim.


## Foreign agents (FARA)

For who represents foreign governments, parties and companies in the US, use `foreign_agents_summary`, `foreign_agents_country`, `foreign_agents_registrant`, `foreign_agents_principal` and `foreign_agents_enforcement`, which answer from MORVS's [Foreign Agents Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/foreign-agents/). Call them with `{}`, `{"country":"Saudi Arabia","year":2025}`, `{"name":"Akin"}` or `{"name":"Public Investment Fund"}`. Registration under FARA is a legal disclosure; it does not show wrongdoing. Amounts are what agents reported receiving on behalf of foreign principals for their own six-month periods, as DOJ prints them, with DOJ's posting date; "None Reported" is not zero. Country names are as registrants entered them and are not a statement of US policy. FARA receipts, LDA filing counts and PAC money are separate records, never added; a firm's PAC gives for the whole firm, not for a foreign client. Enforcement counts are DOJ's own words; give no reason for them. Individuals are withheld. Never say "spy", "propaganda", "influence campaign" or "foreign meddling".

## Inaugural committee and super PAC donors (FEC records)

For organisations that gave to the 2025 inaugural committee, use `inaugural_donors_summary` and `inaugural_donors_donor` ([Inaugural Donors Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/inaugural-donors/)); for organisations' money to super PACs in 2025-26, use `super_pac_donors_summary`, `super_pac_donors_donor` and `super_pac_donors_recipient` ([Super PAC Donors Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/super-pac-donors/)). Call them with `{}`, `{"name":"Microsoft"}`, `{"name":"Coinbase"}` or `{"committee":"Fairshake"}`. These records show donations reported to the FEC; they do not show what any donor received or intended, or that any donation influenced any decision. Accepted, refunded and net amounts are separate; organisation, committee and individual money are separate measures; contracts, lobbying, PAC money and donations are never added. Individuals are counted, never named. Committees are named as the FEC lists them; super PACs carry no party label. Say "nonprofit" only where the answer shows an IRS match. Never "bought", "access", "pay-to-play" or "dark money".
## Migrant shelter, resettlement and legal-help money (award records)

For federal money to organisations and governments that shelter, resettle and give legal help to migrants and refugees, FY2023-26, use `migrant_programs_summary`, `migrant_programs_recipient`, `migrant_programs_terminations` and `migrant_programs_program` ([Migrant Program Money Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/migrant-programs/)). Call them with `{}`, `{"name":"Southwest Key"}`, `{"agency":"FEMA"}` or `{"program":"orr_uc"}`. Obligations, outlays and de-obligations are separate stages and never added; terminations are quoted in the agency's own words, and where the record gives no reason the tools say so. These records show federal awards and the agencies' termination records; they do not show how any recipient used the money or why an award was made or ended beyond what the record says.

## Federal records systems about people (Privacy Act)

For what federal agencies say their databases hold about people, who an agency says may receive the records, and how to request your own file, use the two Federal Files Register tools, which answer from MORVS's published [Federal Files Register](https://morvs.ai/voidly/federal-files/): every Privacy Act system-of-records notice in the 2025 GovInfo compilation, with later Federal Register notices linked. Call `federal_file_search` with `{"query":"Social Security number"}`, `{"agency":"DHS"}`, `{"recipient":"immigration"}` or `{"flag":"biometrics"}`; then call `federal_file_system` with the system's id (for example `{"id":"SSA:60-0058"}`) for its categories of records, routine uses, exemptions, linked notices and the agency's own access, notification and contesting procedures.

A routine use is the agency's statement that it MAY disclose records to a recipient for a purpose; it is not evidence that any disclosure happened, so say "may disclose", never "shares". Recipient tags and record flags are text matches counted as "at least"; standard boilerplate uses are left out unless include_standard is true. A DHS mention does not mean ICE. An exemption claim can limit access or correction but does not decide anyone's request. A missing system is not proof no records are kept. When a person wants their file, give the agency's own request procedure and link the official notice; MORVS never receives requests. If a tool reports the register as temporarily unavailable, say so.
